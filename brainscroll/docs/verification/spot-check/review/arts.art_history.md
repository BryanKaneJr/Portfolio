## Needs your call
### 15. Level 72: Japan's Influence on the West  **SOFTEN**
> Japan had been closed to the West since about 1600. When trade resumed, prints like Level 71's reached Europe.

**Problem:** Japan's seclusion policy dates from the 1630s, not about 1600 (the Dutch arrived in 1600 and the English kept a trading post until 1623), and it was never fully closed: Dutch merchants traded at Dejima throughout. "Closed to the West" leaves the learner thinking there was no Western contact at all for 250 years.
**Proposed:** "From the 1630s Japan had shut out the West, apart from a few Dutch traders at Nagasaki. When wider trade resumed in the 1850s, prints like Level 71's reached Europe."  (appears in: card 072.c1 body; headline "In the 1850s, Japanese pictures started arriving in Europe" is fine as is)
**Evidence:** [Wikipedia, Japonisme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japonisme): "Tokugawa Iemitsu ordered that an island, Dejima, be built off the shores of Nagasaki from which Japan could receive imports. The Dutch were the only Westerners able to engage in trade with the Japanese." The cited [Saint Louis Art Museum page](https://www.slam.org/blog/edgar-degas-in-the-era-of-japonisme/) (would not load; seen via search) supports only "after trade with Japan resumed in the 1850s", not the 1600 start date.
