### 13. Level 64: Greek Astronomy  **SOFTEN**
> If Earth moves, the stars should seem to shift as you watch from different points on its path. Nobody could see any shift, and backers of the Sun-centered idea couldn't explain why. So it won little support.

**Problem:** Aristarchus did offer an explanation: the stars are so far away that the shift is too small to see. The trouble was that this required a vastly bigger universe than anyone accepted and could not be tested. "Couldn't explain why" misstates this.
**Proposed:** "Nobody could see any shift. Aristarchus's answer was that the stars are immensely far away, but that meant a universe far bigger than anyone believed, and no one could test it. So the idea won little support."  (appears in: card 064.c4 body; question 064.q2 explanation, suggest "A moving Earth should make the stars seem to shift; no one could see it, and the only answer, that the stars were immensely far away, couldn't be tested.")
**Evidence:** [Aristarchus of Samos (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristarchus_of_Samos): per Archimedes' Sand Reckoner, Aristarchus handled the problem by "postulating that the stars were other suns that are very far away, far enough that the parallax was not observable." [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Aristarchus-of-Samos): a moving Earth should produce a stellar shift "unless" the stars are extraordinarily distant; Archimedes noted the theory needed a universe "vastly larger than was then believed."

### 17. Level 81: Philip of Macedon  **SOFTEN**
> Philip rebuilt his army around the sarissa, a pike nearly one and a half times as long as the spear other Greeks carried.

**Problem:** Britannica does use the "one and a half times" phrase, but the cited source and standard figures put the sarissa at roughly 5 to 6 m against a hoplite spear of about 2 to 3 m, i.e. about twice as long. "Half again as long" understates the key point of the lesson.
**Proposed:** "a pike roughly twice as long as the spear other Greeks carried" and, in the analogy, "your opponent's pole is twice as long as yours"  (appears in: card 081.c3 body, twice; question 081.q1 option b "A pike about twice as long as a Greek spear" and its explanation; question 090.q3 explanation)
**Evidence:** [Philip II of Macedon (World History Encyclopedia)](https://www.worldhistory.org/Philip_II_of_Macedon/): "the sarissa, an 18 to 20 feet pike"; [Sarissa (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarissa): "about 5 to 7 meters (16 to 23 ft) in length"; [Dory (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dory_(spear)): the hoplite spear "was about 2 to 3 meters in length."
