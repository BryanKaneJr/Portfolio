### 6. Level 28: Making New Elements  **FIX**
> In 2005, in Dubna, Russia, a Russian and American team fired calcium ions at a californium target at about 10 percent of the speed of light. [...] From February to June, the experiment produced three atoms.

**Problem:** The three element 118 atoms came from two runs, not one: a single decay chain in a 2002 run and two more in the February to June 2005 run, all reported together in the 2006 paper. The cited LLNL press release credits all three to 2005, but the primary paper and later reviews do not. The fusion arithmetic (20 + 98 = 118), the place, the team and the beam speed are all correct.
**Proposed:** "At Dubna, Russia, a Russian and American team fired calcium ions at a californium target at about 10 percent of the speed of light. [...] Runs in 2002 and 2005 produced three atoms in all." Fact text: "Element 118 was made at Dubna, Russia (runs in 2002 and 2005, reported in 2006), by a Russian and American team who..."  (appears in: card 028.c3 body; fact text. Card 029.c4 "three atoms" and question 028.q2 are fine as they stand.)
**Evidence:** [Thoennessen, Discovery of Isotopes of Elements with Z >= 100 (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.2105): Oganessian et al. reported 294118 in the 2006 paper; "One of the three measured decay chains had been mentioned in a previous publication by the same group". The 2006 paper ([Phys. Rev. C 74, 044602](https://journals.aps.org/prc/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevC.74.044602)) describes a 2002 run of 2,300 hours that gave a single chain, plus a later irradiation. The [LLNL release](https://www.llnl.gov/article/30421/livermore-scientists-team-russia-discover-element-118) says the February to June 2005 experiments "produced three atoms", which is where the card's wording comes from.

### 14. Level 64: Neutralization  **SOFTEN**
> A salt is any compound built from ions, a positive ion other than H+ and a negative ion other than OH-. Every acid-base neutralization makes a salt plus water.

**Problem:** The cited textbook says neutralization products are "often" a salt and water, not always. For example, ammonia plus hydrochloric acid gives ammonium chloride and no water. The salt definition itself is fine.
**Proposed:** "A typical acid-base neutralization makes a salt plus water."  (appears in: card 064.c3 body; fact text. Question 064.q1 and its explanation are fine for the hydroxide bases this level uses.)
**Evidence:** [OpenStax Chemistry 2e, 4.2 Classifying Chemical Reactions](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/4-2-classifying-chemical-reactions): "A neutralization reaction is a specific type of acid-base reaction in which the reactants are an acid and a base (but not water), and the products are often a salt and water."
