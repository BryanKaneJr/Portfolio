# Earth, Weather & Climate: tree rules (settled science, neutral on policy)
- Teach the established science plainly. On climate change, follow the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6, 2021 to 2023) and NASA, NOAA and the UK Met Office: the world has warmed, the warming since the mid-1800s is caused mainly by human greenhouse gas emissions, and it is already changing heat, rainfall and ice. Don't hedge settled findings with "some say", and don't overstate: keep the IPCC's own likelihood words ("likely", "very likely") and ranges (for example climate sensitivity likely 2.5 to 4 °C) and say when a figure is a projection, not a measurement.
- Policy is described, never judged. Level 98 (Paris and the Climate Debate) and any mention of responses present options (carbon pricing, regulation, subsidies, nuclear, renewables, adaptation, geoengineering research) as choices people weigh, with the main argument on each side, attributed to "supporters" and "critics" or to named bodies. Never favor or disparage a party, country, politician, company or movement, and never tell the learner what to do or think about policy. National participation in agreements changes (the US has joined and left the Paris Agreement more than once): state the agreement's goals, and avoid current-status claims unless dated and sourced.
- Numbers are dated and sourced: global temperature and CO2 figures with the year and the agency (NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory for CO2, NASA GISS, NOAA NCEI, Copernicus or the WMO for temperature), USGS for earthquakes, volcanoes and geologic time, the International Commission on Stratigraphy for the time scale, the NWS and NHC for storm scales and safety advice, FEMA and Ready.gov for preparedness. Say "about" where sources round. Prefer long-term averages to single record years, which date fast.
- Safety advice (Levels 93 to 97, and any hazard level) must match current official US guidance: Drop, Cover and Hold On (not the "triangle of life" or standing in a doorway); "Turn Around, Don't Drown"; "When thunder roars, go indoors"; tornado shelter in an interior room on the lowest floor, never opening windows; heat illness warning signs per the CDC. Give general guidance only, never medical advice, and never make a hazard sound thrilling. Describe past disasters with respect for the people affected; no graphic detail and no death-toll one-upmanship.
- Keep uncertainty honest where science is genuinely open: earthquakes cannot be predicted (only forecast as probabilities); tipping points and the exact pace of ice-sheet loss carry wide ranges; the link between warming and individual tornadoes is still weak, while the link to extreme heat and heavy rain is strong. Don't turn open questions into certainties either way.
- Overlaps, go deeper and don't repeat: World Geography already teaches Inside the Earth, Plate Tectonics, Pangaea, the basics of earthquakes, volcanoes and the Ring of Fire, tsunamis, How Mountains Rise, the Rock Cycle, erosion in general, the Water Cycle, Weather or Climate?, Why the Equator Is Hot, Wind and Pressure (trade winds), rain shadows, monsoons, the basics of hurricanes and of thunderstorms and tornadoes, climate zones, a first look at the greenhouse effect, and biomes. Build on these in a phrase ("You met the rock cycle in World Geography") and teach the next layer down; never re-teach them as the level's main idea.
- The Oceans owns ocean currents, the Gulf Stream, El Niño, the ocean's heat and carbon, rising seas, storm surges, tsunamis, sea ice and icebergs; Chemistry owns carbon dating, acid rain, ocean acidification, photosynthesis, fuels as energy stores and crystals as chemistry; Astronomy owns why we have seasons (tilt), the Moon and tides, Earth's magnetic field and auroras; The Animal Kingdom owns how fossils form, dinosaurs and the five mass extinctions. Mention these in a phrase where needed, register no concept for them and never write a question on them. Don't contradict them: check a shared figure against that tree before using it.
- Common mistakes to avoid: the ozone hole is not the main cause of global warming; weather is not climate (one cold week proves nothing either way); most rain outside the tropics starts as ice (Level 54 should say so, since warm tropical clouds can rain without ice); magnitude is not intensity; a hurricane's category rates wind only; the greenhouse effect itself is natural and essential, the change is its strengthening; carbon dating can't date rocks or dinosaurs; lightning can and does strike the same place twice.
- Level 100 art is `earth.mastery`. Never put the same image on two consecutive levels.
