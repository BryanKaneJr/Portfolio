# Ancient Greece: tree rules
- Dates approximate ("about"); prefer Britannica, the British Museum, the Met, Perseus (Tufts), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, university classics pages.
- Myths are told as myths ("the Greeks told..."); legendary figures and events are flagged as legend.
- Slavery and women's exclusion are stated plainly, without minimizing; Athenian democracy was for adult male citizens only.
- Contested numbers (Thermopylae's "300", Persian army sizes, battle dates) are given as ranges with the ancient source named (Herodotus's figures are known to be exaggerated).
- Sparta: separate evidence from later myth-making (much comes from non-Spartan writers like Plutarch).
- Philosophers' ideas are attributed; Socrates is known mainly through Plato and Xenophon.
- Overlaps: Art History Levels 14–16 (vases, sculpture, temples) and Architecture (Parthenon, column orders), Egypt Levels 85–87 (Alexander, Ptolemies, the Library). Do not contradict them; keep the Greek angle.
