# World Religions: tree rules (describe, never judge)
- Religion is taught here as ideas, practice and history, beside Philosophy, never as mythology. Beliefs are always what adherents believe, attributed to them: "Muslims believe the Quran was revealed to Muhammad through the angel Gabriel", "Christians believe Jesus rose from the dead", "Sikhs believe", "many Hindus believe". Never state a belief as plain fact, and never call any faith, text or teaching true, false, primitive, advanced, better or worse, not even by implication ("claims", "supposedly", "legend" for a living faith's sacred story). Historical claims are what historians accept, said with the right strength: "most historians accept that Jesus was a Jewish teacher crucified under Pontius Pilate around 30 CE"; "archaeology has not confirmed the Exodus as the Bible describes it" (Level 12); the Buddha's dates are uncertain (5th or 4th century BCE); the Vedas were composed from about 1500 BCE (scholars' estimate). Where a tradition's own dating differs from scholars', give both, attributed. Use BCE and CE.
- Comparable depth and respect. Each tradition gets its own voice, its inner variety and its best-known practices, told as its members would recognize them. Never define one faith by contrast with another ("unlike Christianity, Hinduism lacks..."). Use each community's own names: Latter-day Saints and the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (not "Mormon Church"), Hebrew Bible or Tanakh (say "Old Testament" only when describing Christian use), Vodou (never "voodoo"), Santería (also called Lucumí or Regla de Ocha), Baha'i, Guru Nanak and the Gurus with the title, "Allah" explained as the Arabic word for God (Arab Christians use it too). No stereotypes: never link a faith to violence, poverty or backwardness; show that every tradition has many voices (Level 4's lesson), use "many" or "most" rather than "all" adherents, and show women and ordinary believers, not only founders and clergy. Humor is fine about the learner, never about a belief, a practice or a believer.
- Sensitive levels, stated factually, briefly and with sources: 14 (the Temple's destruction), 38 (Sunni and Shia: a leadership dispute and its history, no ranking of branches, no present-day conflicts), 72 (African faiths under slavery), 86 (caste: a social hierarchy India's 1950 Constitution banned discrimination by; many Hindus reject it as religious; present Ambedkar's critique and Hindu reformers' views, never caste as the essence of Hinduism), 88 (persecution: Roman persecution of Christians, the expulsions of Jews from Spain in 1492, the Holocaust with about six million Jews murdered, and the persecution of Baha'is in Iran, all in the past tense or as documented by the UN; then Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights), 95 (war: weigh named scholars' explanations, such as William Cavanaugh's argument that "religious violence" is a misleading category against Mark Juergensmeyer's studies of religious motives; no single percentage of "religious wars"), 97 (new religious movements: explain the scholars' term and how to judge a group by its actions; never label any current group a cult). No conflicts after 2001, no current politicians, no views on the State of Israel beyond its founding in 1948 in a phrase, and no attacks named as a faith's acts. "Jihad", if it comes up, is explained as scholars and most Muslims define it (striving, with armed struggle one narrow, regulated sense), in a phrase.
- Indigenous and African traditions (Levels 67 to 69, 72) are living religions, told in the present tense. Name the nation or people (Lakota, Diné, Haudenosaunee, Anangu, Yoruba), say that practices differ between nations, and never merge them into one "Native religion". Don't describe ceremonies or knowledge that the community keeps restricted or secret, and don't teach how to perform a ceremony (smudging, sweat lodge, divination). Avoid "primitive", "tribal religion", "witch doctor", "animism" as a label for a people, and "Dreamtime" except as an older term; prefer "the Dreaming" and "Country". Use the nation's or community's own official sites where they exist.
- Numbers come from the Pew Research Center, with the data year: the world in 2020 (Pew, June 2025) was about 29 percent Christian, 26 percent Muslim, 24 percent religiously unaffiliated, 15 percent Hindu and 4 percent Buddhist, with about 15 million Jews; the US Religious Landscape Study (2023 to 2024) found about 62 percent Christian, 29 percent unaffiliated and about 7 percent other faiths. Say "about"; the US Census does not ask about religion. Other sources: Britannica, the Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Religion, Harvard's Pluralism Project, university religious studies departments and university presses; each faith's own official bodies for how it describes itself (for example the Vatican, bahai.org, the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee), always attributed ("the Church teaches"); the National Archives and the Library of Congress for American documents; the UN for the Universal Declaration. Scripture is quoted only from public-domain translations (the King James Version, the 1917 Jewish Publication Society Tanakh), named; otherwise paraphrase. Advocacy sites of any side, apologetics sites and anti-religion sites are never sources. Wikipedia for discovery only.
- Level art: objects and buildings only (`images.md`). Never an image of Muhammad or any prophet; no figures of Jesus, Mary, the Buddha, the Gurus, saints or Hindu deities (no statues or icons of them either); no people at worship; no emblems drawn on their own (cross, crescent and star, Star of David, Om, khanda, dharma wheel, yin-yang); no scripture with visible letters. Cards may describe images a tradition uses (icons, murtis) in words.
- Overlaps: don't reteach and don't contradict. Ancient Rome owns early Christianity's spread, its persecution, Constantine and Theodosius (Rome 83 to 88): Level 21 is about Jesus himself and Level 23 mentions Nicaea in a phrase. The Middle Ages owns Islam's first-century spread (MA 8), the medieval Church, monasteries, pilgrimage in Europe, the Schism of 1054, the Inquisition, the Crusades and the Islamic golden age of science (MA 7, 31 to 49, 61 to 65): Level 31 teaches the Prophet's life and message, not conquests, and Level 27 recalls 1054 in a phrase. Ancient Egypt and Ancient Greece own their gods; Literature owns mythology, including the Mahabharata and Ramayana as epics (Level 45 teaches the Bhagavad Gita as scripture only). Art History (19, 21 to 27, 73) and Architecture (22 to 30) own religious art and buildings: Level 84 explains the religious reasons for avoiding images and recalls Byzantine icons and Islamic pattern in a phrase. Philosophy owns philosophers and arguments for and against God's existence, the problem of evil, and Confucius's and Laozi's ideas as philosophy: Levels 62 and 63 teach Confucian and Daoist rites, temples and practice. How Government Works owns theocracy (Gov 15) and freedom of religion as a right in general (Gov 35): Level 74 teaches the American history of the religion clauses (the Virginia Statute of 1786, Article VI's ban on religious tests, Jefferson's 1802 Danbury letter), Level 91 compares secular states. World Geography 95 owns where religions are practiced: Level 9 teaches how believers are counted. US History owns the Puritans (US 12), Spanish missions (US 8), enslaved people's faith (US 44) and the civil rights movement's events (US 83, 84): Levels 75 and 78 teach the religious movements and recall those in a phrase. Music owns Gregorian chant, spirituals and reggae (Music 24, 61, 86).
- Tense and "today": traditions are described as they are practiced now, but no post-2001 events, and no current leaders by name except in an office's description if needed (say "the pope", not a name). Dates in full for anything tested (1517, 1699, 1844). Spell names consistently: Quran, sharia, Guru Granth Sahib, Baha'u'llah, Siddhartha Gautama, Mahavira.
