# Government: tree rules (strict neutrality)
- Explain how government works, not what it should do. Contested questions are views held by named thinkers, parties' own published positions in general terms, or traditions, never the app's view. No partisan framing; never favor or disparage any party, country, current politician or movement.
- Examples from many countries (not only the US); when describing the US system (Chapter 4's U.S. Constitution), be factual.
- Figures are official (OECD, IMF, UN, national statistics offices, Congress.gov, UK Parliament, Library of Congress) with the year.
- Sensitive topics (civil rights, suffrage, protest, police, prisons, immigration/refugees, misinformation) stated factually with sources; describe debates as debates between named sides.
- "Spotting Misinformation": general media-literacy methods (lateral reading, checking sources), no examples tied to current partisan disputes.
- Overlaps: Middle Ages (Magna Carta, early parliaments), Ancient Greece (Athenian democracy), Money (taxes, budgets). Do not contradict them.
