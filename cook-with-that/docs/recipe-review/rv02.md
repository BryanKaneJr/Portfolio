# rv02 review: beef and pork/seafood originals

## Summary
- **Reviewed:** 51 recipes (25 in `src/data/originals/beef.ts`, 26 in `src/data/originals/porkSeafood.ts`). I read every
  one in full and checked amounts, "divided" splits, pan sizes, times, doneness, ingredient IDs, tags and taglines.
  I also looked at all 9 audit flags.
- **Fixed:** 6 recipes, each with a small edit. No ids, titles, taglines or tags changed, and no catalog additions.
- **Questions for the owner:** 5 recipes: fish and chips, the two steaks, the lamb chops and the empanadas. There's
  also one house-style point that covers 5 beef rice recipes.
- Overall these two files are in good shape. Every "divided" amount adds up, every step's ingredient is on the list,
  ground meat says 160°F, pork and fish say 145°F, and every oven step says when to preheat.
- `patch.diff` was made with the brief's command, so its paths are relative to the repo root
  (`a/cook-with-that/src/data/originals/...`).

## Fixed
- `swedish-meatballs`: the step said "1-inch balls, about 1 tbsp each. You should get around 36". Those three numbers
  don't agree. The mix weighs about 2 lb (960 g), which makes ~55–60 level-tablespoon balls, ~100 one-inch balls, and
  only 36 if each ball is about 2 tbsp. A cook would end up with far more meatballs than promised. → "1 1/4-inch balls,
  about 1 heaping tbsp each. You should get around 50." That keeps them small, matching the tagline, and the
  two-batch browning and 8–10 minute times still work.
- `classic-pot-roast-with-carrots-and-potatoes`: the gravy used a paste of 2 tbsp butter and 2 tbsp flour for the
  pot juices from 2 cups of broth plus a 3 1/2 lb braised roast, roughly 2 1/2–3 cups. That is under 1 tbsp of flour per
  cup, which gives a thin jus, not the "rich gravy" in the tagline. → 3 tbsp butter and 3 tbsp flour (about 1 tbsp per
  cup). The step already whisks the paste in "a little at a time", so the cook can stop when it's thick enough.
- `steak-tacos-with-pico-de-gallo`: the marinade said "30 minutes at room temperature, or up to 8 hours in the fridge".
  USDA says to always marinate in the refrigerator. 30 minutes on the counter is inside the 2-hour rule, but fridge
  marinating costs nothing here. → "Cover and marinate in the fridge for at least 30 minutes and up to 8 hours".
  prepMinutes (50) still fits.
- `garlic-herb-pork-tenderloin`: after the skillet came off the burner, the next step only said "Roast 10–15 minutes",
  without saying to put the pan in the oven. → "Move the skillet to the oven and roast…", plus a reminder that the
  handle stays hot, since a beginner will grab it on the stovetop afterwards.
- `mussels-in-white-wine-and-garlic`: no pot size was given ("your largest pot") for 4 lb of mussels, which need room
  to open. → "a large pot with a lid (8-quart or bigger; the mussels need room to open)". The metric display
  converts "8-quart".
- `sausage-and-white-bean-casserole`: the crusty bread is optional on the list, but the step read "serve with crusty
  bread" as if it were required. → "Scatter parsley over and serve with crusty bread, if you like."

## For the owner
- `beer-battered-fish-and-chips`: **no dish type** (audit flag). Nothing in the current list honestly fits. It's
  deep-fried in a pot, so it isn't a Skillet, Casserole/Bake or one-dish meal, and tagging it One-Pot would mislead
  someone who filters for easy one-pot meals. Recommendation: leave it untagged for now. If more fried dishes arrive
  (churros, fried chicken), consider a "Fried" dish type.
- `steak-with-garlic-butter-and-pan-potatoes` and `steak-tacos-with-pico-de-gallo`: **doneness choice.** Both offer
  "130°F (54°C) for medium-rare, or 145°F (63°C) for medium", and the first notes that 145°F is the USDA minimum.
  Recommendation: keep both options as written, since this is the standard way to present whole-cut beef. If you'd
  rather show only USDA temperatures, change 130 to 145 in those two steps.
- `mongolian-beef` and `pepper-steak-with-rice`: the audit flagged them for having no internal temperature. They use
  1/4-inch stir-fry strips cooked until browned, which the brief allows to use a visual cue. Recommendation: no
  change.
- `rosemary-garlic-lamb-chops` (to check when cooking): the time to baste to 145°F, "2–3 minutes" after a 3–4 minute
  first side, looks short for 1 1/4-inch loin chops; I'd expect 4–6 minutes. The thermometer cue still decides when
  they're done, so it's safe. Recommendation: whoever cooks it should note the real time and update the step.
- `beef-empanadas` (to check when cooking): "a heaping 1/4 cup of the filling" on a 5-inch round is generous. It's
  needed to use up the ~3 1/2–4 cups of filling across 12 rounds, but it may be hard for a beginner to seal.
  Recommendation: whoever cooks it should check this. If it's tight, use 6-inch rounds (the dough is enough at 1/8 inch).
- **House style, rice (5 beef recipes):** `korean-ground-beef-rice-bowls`, `ground-beef-and-broccoli-stir-fry`,
  `ground-beef-burrito-bowls-with-black-beans`, `mongolian-beef` and `pepper-steak-with-rice` say "Cook the rice as
  the package directs" with no water amount or time. The chicken originals spell it out ("2 1/2 cups water, boil,
  cover, low 18 minutes, rest 10"). It works for anyone with a package, so I left it. Recommendation: decide on one
  house rule. If spelled-out rice is preferred, add a water line and the stovetop method to these five.
- Near-duplicate flags, all checked and **not** duplicates (different protein or a different dish):
  `beef-and-black-bean-enchiladas` / cheesy-chicken-enchiladas, `ground-beef-burrito-bowls-with-black-beans` /
  chicken-burrito-bowls, `classic-pot-roast-with-carrots-and-potatoes` / irish-beef-stew (a whole roast vs. a cubed
  stew), `pulled-pork` / `oven-baked-bbq-ribs`, `lamb-gyros-with-tzatziki` / chicken-souvlaki-with-pita. No drops
  recommended.
- Short resting at room temperature before searing (steak in `steak-with-garlic-butter-and-pan-potatoes` while the
  potatoes cook, about 20 minutes; `rosemary-garlic-lamb-chops`, 20 minutes) and the 30-minute batter rest in
  `toad-in-the-hole` are all well inside the 2-hour rule. Left as written.
